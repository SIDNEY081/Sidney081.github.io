SafeShell

A one-tap decoy home screen that hides your banking apps if you're ever forced to hand over your phone

Live Demo Source Code

The problem

If someone forces you to unlock your phone - a robbery, a hijacking - your banking apps are usually one home-screen tap away. SafeShell exists for that specific moment: a way to hand over a phone that looks normal but doesn't give an attacker a route to your money.

How it works

SafeShell replaces the phone's home screen with a custom Android launcher. Under duress, switching into the decoy desktop is a single tap - deliberately frictionless, because a multi-step gesture or a PIN you have to think about isn't realistic when someone is threatening you. Once active, the decoy launcher hides banking apps entirely and blocks access to the Play Store, so an attacker can't install something to work around what's missing either.

Why the asymmetry matters

Getting into the decoy is instant on purpose; getting back out isn't. Returning to the real home screen and your actual apps requires the app's Firebase-backed authentication with biometric login (fingerprint or face). That asymmetry is the actual security property: an attacker holding the phone can't simply "undo" the decoy even if they suspect it's there, because reversing it needs your fingerprint or face, not just persistence.

Built on

Kotlin, with the launcher-replacement behavior as the core custom logic - overriding what Android treats as the device's home screen isn't a standard app feature, it's built specifically for this. Authentication runs on Firebase Auth with the Android Biometric API, and the interface uses Material Design 3 components. Minimum SDK 26 (Android 8.0), targeting SDK 34 (Android 14).

Where it stands

The authentication and biometric-login flow is documented and working end to end. The decoy-launcher behavior described above - the one-tap switch, hiding banking apps, blocking the Play Store - is the actual design and functionality of the app; it isn't yet written up in the project's own README, which currently only documents the login/biometric side.
